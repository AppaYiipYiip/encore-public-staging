They are CSS custom properties on body, named like tokens.dart (--bg, --surface, --surface-high, --outline, --on-bg, --on-bg-muted, --on-bg-faint, --accent, --on-accent, --danger, --warning, radii, spacing, motion). Added by this direction: --accent-ink, --outline-strong, --focus, --warning-wash, --danger-wash, --scrim, --stage. The cover artwork is generated art and is the only place with colours that are not tokens.

    Motion

    Curve --ease = cubic-bezier(.215,.61,.355,1) (easeOutCubic, as today). Nothing blurs, nothing animates layout, nothing needs a shader. Two things run on their own, both cheap: three equaliser bars (transform: scaleY, 900 ms, only on the song that plays) and the skeleton pulse (opacity, 1.4 s). Everything else is a 120 to 320 ms cross-fade.

    Focus, D-pad and input

    RuleHow it is drawn here
    Focus is always visible on X and Ta 2 to 3 dp outline in --focus with a gap; inside the row on --surface-high for rows and keys. Drawn on one control in every X and T frame (is-focus).
    Focus order on Tnumbered badges 1, 2, 3 (switch "D-pad order" above the frame): left and right move along a shelf, up and down move between shelves, the rail is reached from the left edge, Back always goes up one level.
    No hover, no long pressevery row has a visible "more" button; every action a long press would give is in that menu; nothing appears only on hover.
    Touch on Cevery control is at least 48 x 48 (the viewer runs ?check=1 to list any smaller one); persistent controls cap their small labels (the tabs and the Lyrics, Queue and Devices shortcuts at 1.3x as the stock Flutter NavigationBar does, the player times at 1.5x); everything else, including every sentence and every title, scales to 2.0x. At 200 % on C the Now playing cover is dropped so the title stays on screen.
    Right-to-leftlogical properties only; chevrons and the back arrow mirror; the scrubber, the time labels and the transport controls keep their direction (time runs left to right); uppercase and letter-spacing are off.
    Colours that are not tokensonly the camera view (#161816, white corner marks) and the QR panel (white, near-black): physical objects, the same in both themes.

    Direction B · Quiet editorial

    Why this direction

    1. The ideaSet the music like a well-made page: strong type, hairlines, small covers and almost no colour, so the few things that need you (playing, press this, waiting, broken) are the only things that shine.
    2. Good atSunlight and sofa: 7 to 16 : 1 text contrast, type that holds at 200 % and mirrors cleanly right-to-left, one skeleton for phone, browser and TV, and states that cannot be mistaken (waiting, offline, broken and "another server" each have their own mark, word and colour).
    3. Bad atIt is the least alive of the three (docs/16 §2 wants the music to be the art): a new person sees a calm page, small covers make unknown names slower to scan, and it depends on a bundled serif that must also carry Arabic well.
    4. Flutter cost: lowMostly stock widgets: CustomScrollView + SliverList with hairline Dividers, NavigationBar (C) and NavigationRail (X, T), FocusTraversalGroup with one FocusRing wrapper, Directionality and MediaQuery.textScaler. Custom: EditorialRow, StateStrip, Scrubber (SliderTheme), EqBars (scaleY only), one bundled font family. No blur, no per-frame palette extraction.
    5. The others probably do betterA (cover-led) is more alive in the first five seconds and on the lock screen; C (warm home) welcomes a person who has never run a server better than a page of hairlines does.
