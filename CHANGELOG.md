# Changelog

User-visible changes, newest first. One line per change, in plain words.

## Unreleased

Everything below is built and tested but **not yet walked on a phone** (`docs/tasks/RELEASE-CANDIDATE.md`, the definition of pass); nothing here is released.

* **Year in Music** now counts the plays your server has on record for you, from all your phones, at home and away. Before, on an Encore server it asked the music server for its own list through its own sign-in, which never worked away from home (the phone then showed only the plays it had seen itself) and which your server is about to stop passing on (P3-34). A play only the music server recorded (another music app, plays from before this phone reported to Encore) is not counted any more; nothing is lost, and it can be brought in later. A plain music server without Encore is read as before. (Server and phone; built, not walked.)
* A download you switch off and on again quickly, or stop and resume, can no longer be shown as a song that "did not come" because of the late answer of the attempt you cancelled, and a song that is already downloading is not left stuck: every download attempt on the phone now has a name of its own, so each answer of the downloader is read against the attempt it belongs to (N-45; the phone's database gets one new field; built, not walked; downloads in progress when you update carry on as before).
* **The music server's own web page is now shut unless you switch it on.** Before, anything that looked like it was on your home network could open the music server's own web page, its sign-in and its programming interface through your server's address; on a computer that hides who is visiting (Docker on Windows or a Mac, some network storage boxes, a router that forwards a port to it) that included the internet. Now they answer "not found" to everybody. **If you use that web page:** run `sudo encore addon music-web-page runs --risk-version 1`, then `sudo encore restart`; it then opens only to devices your server can prove are in your house, and stays shut where it cannot tell (the admin pages will say so). Your apps and the Subsonic apps that use `/rest` are not affected, and neither is `/admin`. A link to a Navidrome share (`/share/...`) is shut too. (Server; built, not walked.)
* Your server now locks its secrets with keys of your own. The passwords of the people on your server are stored under a key made for your install (before, every install used the same one, which is public), and every backup is locked so that a copy taken away or stolen cannot be opened: the installer and the new `encore secure` command show you a backup key **once** and keep it nowhere on the computer, and restoring a backup asks for it. **Save that key: without it a backup cannot be opened by anybody.** An existing server gets its password key at the next `encore update` (a backup is made first) and its backup key when you run `sudo encore secure`, which also locks the older backups; a backup you restore with your key becomes the key your next backups are locked with. (P3-30)
* You can now see and change what Encore does for you as one list: songs from YouTube, pictures and search, real albums, the music server, away from home, backups, friends on other servers, and the ones still to come. Each is run by Encore, yours, or off; the choice is kept and takes effect the next time the server starts; a risky one shows its warning first. The admin page for it is being drawn; today it is the command `sudo encore addon list` and `sudo encore addon ID STATE`. (P3-20, P3-06)
* Your server now has the first brick of its admin pages: open `http://<your server>/admin` on your home network and sign in with your owner account. The **People** page is there: who is on your server, an invitation for somebody new (a link, shown once), locking and unlocking a person, removing one after being asked. The other pages (settings, backups, updates) are being built, and none of it exists for anybody who reaches your server from the internet. (P3-06)
* `encore update` now keeps the program that was running (as `before-update`) and writes `update-record.txt`; if the new version does not start, the sentence it prints says how to go back: that program, the old deployment file and the backup made first, together. The installer's last screen now tells you where the admin pages are. (P3-14, P3-30)
* A new server's owner is now made **in a browser**, not at the terminal: the installer ends by showing a link and a short setup code (`http://<your server>:4533/setup?code=7KQ-4MZ`), you open it on any computer or phone on your home network, choose your name, username and password, and land on the admin pages signed in. The code stops anybody else on your network from claiming the server before you, wrong codes cost a wait, and the page closes for good once your account exists (`encore status` shows the link while it is still to do; the `encore` command takes the code out of the settings file afterwards). `install.sh --owner NAME` still makes the account itself, for installs nobody sits at. Whichever way, the installer now always makes the music server's own account first, and the server refuses the music server's "make the first administrator" address from everybody (a music server with no account at all gives its first one to whoever asks).
* The installer now also sets up a small local **helper** (a socket that only your Encore server can open; on an existing install, `sudo encore helper install`), which the admin pages will use to make a backup or switch the away address on or off. It can do those seven things and nothing else (no restore, no update, no commands, no installing or signing in to Tailscale), refuses every other request, makes its own backups in a separate folder that can never push out your nightly ones, and keeps its own record in `/opt/encore/jobs`. The admin site now has **Backups** (when each was made, how big, who made it, **Back up now**, and how to put one back: a command on the server, never a button) and **Away from home** (the address, what Tailscale says, and one button that switches the address on or off for a Tailscale that is already set up; setting it up stays at the computer's terminal), both through that helper. Home says whether the helper is installed.
* Search and "is it already in my library" now read names in Arabic, Persian, Russian, Hebrew, Hindi, Thai, Korean and Japanese (and accents typed as a letter plus a mark) the way the server does: a word with its vowel signs or harakat is one word, the different spellings of an alef or a ye meet, and a name written with a stress mark finds its plain spelling.
* A music disk or network share that is not mounted for a while (or a music server that answers with nothing) no longer makes Encore forget your music: nothing is removed from what it knows when the music server lists less than half of it, a file is forgotten only after it has been missing for a day while the rest is there, and the admin Home and `encore status` say in words that the music folder looks empty or partly missing.
* The admin Home counts "today" from midnight in your own time zone (the installer reads the computer's), not UTC.
* Songs you downloaded for offline play stay on your phone when the server only fixes their tags or their cover: the app now recognises the sound of a song by the audio itself, not by the size of its file, so a retag no longer makes it download the song again. (A phone that was away while a song was retagged fetches that one song once.)
* Songs you save from a YouTube upload whose performer cannot be told (a video titled with no artist in it) no longer add the uploading channel to your Artists list: they are filed and tagged under "Unknown Artist" instead. Only newly saved songs change; the songs already on your disk keep the tags and the folder they have.
* **A forgotten password has a way back.** `sudo encore reset-password NAME` (on the computer where Encore is installed) sets a new password for anybody, signs all of that person's devices out and writes a line in an audit log; for the owner it asks for the **recovery key** shown once at the end of setup (print button included), accepts none if it is lost (the log says so) and gives a new key afterwards. `sudo encore recovery-key` makes a new key, and `encore status` tells an owner who has none (every account made before keys existed) to make one. Nothing here works from a browser or from the internet: being at the computer is the proof.
* The admin site now opens on a **Home** page that says in one sentence how your server is ("Everything is working." or "One thing needs you.") and, below, one plain row for each part: the music server, songs from YouTube, away from home (and how many days its key has left), the disk (and room for about how many songs), the last backup, the version, your people and what was played today (counts only, never titles). Facts only the computer knows (the last backup, the key's end) are told to the server by the `encore` command after a backup, restore, update or `encore secure`; when the computer has not told it, the row says so instead of showing a green tick, and a backup that stopped shows as a thing that needs you even if the computer stopped reporting.
* Your server no longer starts on the data of a newer version. If an older version of Encore is started on data that a newer one changed (a rollback to an old image), it now refuses in one sentence and changes nothing, instead of misreading the data; the way back is a backup made by that older version. (P3-29)
* Your server now has the first brick of its admin pages: open `http://<your server>/admin` on your home network and sign in with your owner account. Nothing else is there yet (the pages for people, settings and backups are being built), and the page does not exist at all for anybody who reaches your server from the internet. (P3-06)
* Your server now holds back an album it is not sure of: it will not file a song under an album when the catalogue's dates cannot tell the original from a re-release, an EP or a compilation, and not when only one album was found for it (these songs wait in "Songs without an album" until a round that reads the artist's whole discography finds exactly one album that carries the song, N-48; about half of them will not); a song matched to an album gets the album, the track number and the picture but no year (the catalogue's dates of old albums are reissue dates); a Host can also switch the album matching off with `ENCORE_ALBUM_PASS=0` (found by the first live run on a real server, 25 Nov 2026).
* Your server no longer keeps another song of the same artist when it looks for a song's video and only the title, the channel and the length are known: before, a different song uploaded on the artist's own channel at the same length could be taken and kept under the right name. The video's title must now be the wanted song's too (found by reading the matcher, N-44). A song whose whole title is a phrase in brackets is found only as a song result.
* Your server now tells your phone as soon as it has stored a song of an import you started (a link or a file of songs), so the phone no longer has to ask every ten seconds how the import is going: that asking used up battery and data for up to half an hour per import. (The app listens too: it asks again every ten seconds only on a server that does not tell it, and it still stops following an import that has not moved for half an hour; found by the review of the import card, N-43.)
* A song that has more than one file on your music server and loses one of them (a copy you deleted) no longer ends up with two "preferred" files, so it plays the one it had and not the newer copy (found while testing the repair of songs glued onto the wrong entry, P2-25; audit B-137).
* A download now has a **Stop** that keeps what is already on your phone: press Stop under a download's line (in Downloaded, on a playlist or album page, or in Settings > Downloads and storage) and the songs on their way stop, the songs on your phone stay and play, and nothing starts again by itself, not when you open the app again, not when Wi-Fi comes back, not the next day. The line says "Stopped · 12 of 217 on this phone", with a Resume where Stop stood that carries on from there, and the songs you add to the list meanwhile wait for Resume. One quiet notification says "Prog snob is stopped" and what stays on your phone. Your server goes on saving what it was asked for (an import has its own Stop). Taking a whole download off your phone (the Remove in its sheet, the trash button in Settings > Downloads and storage, Remove all downloads) now always asks first, and says what is deleted and what stays: before, the sheet's one red row ("Stop and remove download") deleted the files at once. Removing one song from its menu is as it was. A song that something else cut short (for instance the Cancel button of an old download notification) now reads "Interrupted before it finished." (N-39). The question before removing says how many songs are on your phone at the moment you answer, not when you opened the sheet, and Remove also clears away the half-made copy a Stop left behind in the middle of an update of a song. A release you stopped and then asked for one song of by itself still offers Resume for the rest, and pressing Stop and Resume quickly several times no longer makes the resumed songs fail or start more at once than the limit. A song that stops being wanted by a download that goes on (you removed its own download after stopping the album) no longer keeps coming by itself, and a song that was being made again at a better quality when you pressed Stop is recorded at the quality it really is, so Resume does not fetch it a second time.

* Nothing changes on your phone yet, but your server has learned to decide which album a song of yours really is on, so that "Songs without an album" can empty in the next steps of the same work (N-34). It takes a song for a catalogue entry only when the artist, the title and the length (within 3 seconds) agree and the entry's title is not marked live, a remix, an acoustic or another version when yours is not (labels such as Remastered, Clean, Radio Edit or Explicit are ignored); a cover by another artist, an album credited to somebody else (a tribute, "Various Artists") and a release the catalogue does not call an album, an EP or a single are refused, and a name it cannot compare safely (one with no letter and no digit in it, only symbols, emoji or marks, or one that is nothing but a label such as VEVO or Audio) is skipped, never guessed. Names are compared in the alphabet they are written in (Arabic, Cyrillic and every other alphabet are compared, because of the work in the bullet below) and nothing is transliterated: an Arabic title and the catalogue's Latin spelling of it are two different names, so such a song finds no album, never a wrong one. What it cannot tell by name: a live upload whose title and length are the studio song's (it is taken for the studio song), and a best-of by the same artist that the catalogue calls an album (what is done with it is in the next bullet). The 3-second length margin was measured on 24 Nov 2026 over a real library and its kept files (the numbers are in the body of pull request 125; there was no natural edge), and 3 seconds is kept because a wider margin could write a wrong album for good (`python -m encore_server.album_dryrun`, which only reads a copy of your data and changes nothing; the answers it saves are named by the whole question asked, so a library in any alphabet can be reported on).
* Nothing changes on your phone yet, but your server now looks up once an hour which album the songs that have none are on, and writes what the catalogue says (N-34, second step; built, not walked, and not on any server yet). A song whose album is empty or the music server's "[Unknown Album]", that somebody has liked or put in a playlist or that your server keeps a file of, is asked about (at most 200 songs per round, one search and the albums it names per song, at the catalogue's own pace); when the catalogue's answer passes the rules of the bullet above, the song gets its album, track number, disc, year, the album's picture and the catalogue's ids, and your phones are told, so the song's line names its album. Only what is empty is filled: an album a song already has is never changed, and "[Unknown Album]" becomes plain "no album" for every song the round looks at. What it does not write, because a wrong album would stay for good (your server files your songs by it in the next steps): a song whose recording came out first as a single and later on an album (the album is taken over an earlier single only inside a window of days that the recordings kept in the project are too few to show, so for now such songs wait; the report `python -m encore_server.album_dryrun --live` measures it on your own library, after the test week, and then it is set), a song whose only album is titled like a compilation (Greatest Hits, Best Of, Collection, Anthology, Retrospective and a few more English words; a best-of called Gold is taken), a song in Arabic, Russian or another alphabet whose only catalogue entries are spelled in Latin letters (it is a different name: no album, remembered), and a song that is the same recording as another song of yours (the same catalogue id, or already on that album: it is left as a pair, nothing is written, and the pair is named in the server's log by ids). A song whose only album is a single (a record of one song) is not given that single as its album either, because it might be an album track whose album the search did not show and a wrong album would stay for good: it waits in "Songs without an album" and is asked again, and a later step (the artist's whole list of releases) is meant to find its album. A song whose only album has a title that says live, remix, acoustic, unplugged, demo, karaoke, re-recorded or "(Taylor's Version)" or another version while the song's own title does not is withheld the same way (a real library showed a studio song given the album of a live record; Remastered, Deluxe, Explicit and the like are not such words). An album the catalogue itself calls "Unknown Album" is never taken as a song's album (it would have been a permanent match with no album), and one song that makes the matching fail in an unexpected way is skipped for that round (named by its id in the log) instead of stopping the songs after it. Each of these is remembered with its reason and asked again after 7 days and whenever the rule gets better; a catalogue that cannot be asked (down, resting, switched off) stops the round and remembers nothing. Careful: an album written to a song is also what your server uses, the next time it keeps that song (a download, a re-keep), for the folder and the tags of the file it writes, so a wrong album would reach your music folder through a later download; that is why the first run of this on a real server waits for a report read on a copy of that server's own data (`python -m encore_server.album_dryrun`, which asks the same songs the round asks). A song that holds an ISRC is never given the album of a catalogue entry with another ISRC. Not yet: your files are not renamed, moved or retagged, so the artist page still shows "Songs without an album" for these songs until the next steps; the length margin of 3 seconds means that songs whose file is longer or shorter than the catalogue's entry stay without an album (asked again every 7 days); songs your server saves from now on are asked their album as they are saved (the bullet after the next one); the owner's own second "Creature" stays a pair. The picture finder now takes a catalogue picture only for the same recording (the same version and about the same length), so a live record's picture is no longer given to a studio song and the other way round; a song whose length is unknown, or more than 3 seconds off the catalogue's (an import's listed length can be), keeps its video's thumbnail or none and is not looked up; a live album no longer takes the studio album's picture; a name made only of symbols (a band called three exclamation marks) is no longer looked up for a picture (it used to find its own), because nothing can be compared safely; a song you add from now on that comes without a picture is looked up by the same rule when it is kept, and gets no catalogue picture when its length is unknown or more than 3 seconds off (the length of the file just downloaded is what is compared at that moment, since the bullet after the next one: it was the length listed with the song); a song the round above found gets its picture from that album, and no file or folder picture is written by this step.
* Nothing changes on your phone yet, but the songs your server saves from now on are saved under their album and tagged with it from the start, when the catalogue can place them (N-34, third step; built, not walked, and not on any server yet). When your server has downloaded a song that has no album, it asks the catalogue which album the song is on, by the same rules as the hourly round above (the same recording by name and version, and the length of the file just downloaded within 3 seconds), and only an album that passes them is used: the file goes into `Encore/<artist>/<album>/` as `NN - Title`, is tagged with the album, the track, the disc, the year and the album's picture, and the album's folder gets its `cover.jpg` if it has no picture. Anything else (the catalogue does not know the song, knows it only as a single, finds the song already on that album under another entry of yours, cannot be asked or is slow) leaves the song where it is saved today, in `Singles` with no album, and the hourly round asks again later; a catalogue that cannot be reached is skipped at once, a slow one can hold a download for about ten seconds plus one request's own timeout (8 seconds) before the song is saved without an album, and nothing is remembered about the song in either case. A song that already has an album keeps it, and your own music folder is never written into. A song whose file was already saved is no longer downloaded and saved a second time because its album became known in between (a bug found on 7 Nov); the extra copies it already left on a disk are not removed by this step. Nothing already saved is moved or retagged yet. Careful: the folder and the tags are written on the disk, so before this runs on a server whose music you care about, read what it would do: `python -m encore_server.album_dryrun --new-keeps` over a copy of that server's data shows the folder each song that has no file yet would be saved in.
* A download that is stopped by something other than you removing it (for instance Cancel on a download notification left over from an older version of the app, right after an update) no longer hangs as "downloading" for ever and starts again by itself the next time you open the app: the song is named among the songs that did not come, with Retry, the download's card ends, the next song takes its place, and it is tried again by itself only after a wait that grows (10 minutes, then 20, up to 6 hours). A song you already had keeps playing while its new copy is made again. There is still no Stop button that keeps what is on your phone and waits for you (N-39); turning a download off still removes its songs at once.
* A download or an import now has ONE quiet notification for the whole job, from the first second, not only for the copying to your phone: "Downloading Prog snob: 12 of 217 on this phone · 40 ready on your server", with a bar that only moves forward for as long as the card is there (when one of several downloads finishes the bar does not drop) and is full only when every song is on your phone. While Encore is open it follows the numbers; the moment you leave it the card says what your server had ready and when ("150 of 214 were ready at 9:03. Open Encore to see how far it is now."), which stays true even if the phone then stops Encore. Several downloads are one card; a finished one leaves a card of its own ("Prog snob is ready offline" only when every song is on your phone; "3 songs did not come"; "Import stopped" when you stopped it on another device). A single song gets no card. It is silent, never takes your lock screen over, and is posted only if you allowed notifications (the question is now asked at your first Import as well as your first Download, once). The old "Some of your music is ready offline" card of the downloader is gone. What it costs, said plainly: with Encore closed the copy to your phone stops after the few songs already on their way, and the card outside the app shows no live reason ("waiting for Wi-Fi", "resting until"); a download that carries on after you close the app is a separate item. An import your server calls running but that has not changed for half an hour is no longer followed: Encore stops asking about it every few seconds and the card goes away, without a word (the import page still says what your server says); two endings are not said in a notification yet: an import that failed (its card just goes away), and an import you stopped on another device while you download its playlist here (the download's card goes on and says nothing about the import). Right after the update one old "Some of your music is ready offline" card may still appear once, from files the previous version had already handed over.
* Songs in Arabic, Russian, Korean, Japanese and every other alphabet are now told apart by your server, and so are songs with letters like the Polish ł, the Turkish ı, the German ß and the Scandinavian ø: before, your server dropped every letter outside a to z when it compared songs, so two different songs of one artist in Arabic or Russian were taken for ONE song (the second title was lost, and an exact Arabic result from YouTube Music was refused). The same song written with and without its vowel marks, or with a curly and a straight apostrophe, is now one song. At the first start of the new version your server rewrites how it names its songs (after a copy of its database; likes, playlists, plays and downloads are not touched, and nothing is merged or deleted); songs your music server's library holds in another alphabet that were already merged by the old comparison are listed for a second look and put right at your server's next look at its music (the next entry). A picture the old comparison gave to every Arabic song is dropped and looked up again.
* Songs of your music library that the old comparison glued together are now put right: if your server held five of your Arabic and Russian songs as ONE song (the Fairuz album said "1 song" and it was a Russian one), its hourly look at your music now gives every file the song its own tags name, so the song list of the album shows all its songs. Only the link between a song and its file changes: your files are not renamed, moved, retagged or deleted, and what you liked, put in a playlist, played or queued stays with the song it was on, as you saw it (the old comparison never recorded which song you meant, so nothing is moved to another). The first change is preceded by a copy of the database, and the pairs of songs that share a file are merged afterwards by the usual rules. Your phone learns it from the list of files it already reads: the album's song list is complete, and a download of the song whose file changed is fetched again. A song that the old comparison had already merged with another stays merged, and songs you only ever streamed are not touched. A file is moved only when nothing says it may stay: a song whose file carries no readable tags at all (or no artist), a song that fits the file's tags as well as another song does (a duplicate), and a song you liked, put in a playlist or played whose only file would be taken away are left where they are on purpose (a wrong move is worse than none), and the server keeps that song on its list for a second look (and does not merge it with a duplicate of itself): a file with no tags, or with no artist name, is freed by tagging or removing it; a song that matches two entries equally well, or whose length differs, may stay on that list for good on your server, and the check `audit_stack.py check rekey` names those files and says FAIL for as long as they are there. The look decides only after it has read your whole music library, against the library as it is then, so it finishes in one go (a second look over an unchanged library changes nothing and takes no copy of the database), and a song you liked keeps its file even when the only other file it had is one your music server no longer lists. A look that stops half way (the music server stopped answering) changes nothing and is done again. A step that did not finish (the music server stopped answering, the server was restarted, the final merge failed) is picked up again by the next hourly look, not only after a restart. A library that was never damaged is read and left as it is.
* A server you start by hand (not from the Docker image) now needs Python 3.12 and says so when it is started on another version, instead of starting and naming songs in a way the Docker image would not (the way songs are named depends on the Python's own Unicode tables, so the version is fixed). If you run Encore from the Docker image, nothing changes.
* Music you add to your server's folder is now taken into Encore about a minute after the music server has finished scanning it, not at the next hourly round (it was up to an hour later: 47 minutes in the test with 7,200 files). A big library is not worked over again and again: after a long pass the server rests nine times as long, and a pass never starts while a scan is running. The phone still picks the new songs up on its own schedule (up to ten minutes while the app is open) and the artist and album lists up to an hour later (a follow-up is on the board, P3-35); and on a network share the music server itself may not notice new files until its own hourly scan.
* Search now finds a band whose name you have mistyped AND not finished ("landmrvk" for LANDMVRKS), also offline, and puts it at the top.
* Liked Songs now has a "Find in Liked Songs" field, like the "Find in playlist" field of a playlist: tap it, type a word, and the list shows only the songs you have liked that match (by title, artist or album, found the way Search finds, so accents and other scripts work, and also with no connection). A tap on a found song plays it from its place in the whole of Liked Songs, so the songs you liked before and after it follow, not only the ones that were found; a song your server cannot get stays grey as on the page; the arrow or Back brings the whole list back, and the page is where you left it (N-32).
* An anime opening or ending you import from YouTube ("DAN DA DAN Opening | Otonoke by Creepy Nuts", uploaded by the channel Crunchyroll) is now filed under the artist the title names after "by" (Creepy Nuts, the song "Otonoke"), no longer under the channel that uploaded it. Titles that only contain the words "by", "live", "op" or "ed" are left alone (249 real and hand-written titles are checked by a test, and the rule was refused wherever it changed one that was already right). A concert upload such as "Sepultura, live at Hellfest Open Air 2026 – ARTE Concert" still shows its channel as the artist, on purpose: the same form is also a song uploaded by its own performer, and the title cannot tell them apart.
* Pausing a song that is on your phone while you are offline says nothing about the connection (checked on a phone with Wi-Fi and data off, and with the server stopped; three tests now keep it that way). The coloured bar above the tabs is the colour of the cover of the song playing, not a warning.
* Typing the start of a band's name in Search ("landmvrk" for LANDMVRKS) now gives the band at the top, not a song of an old import whose title repeats the band's name.
* The songs of a playlist you deleted, of a song you took out of a playlist, and of an import you made are now cleaned away from your server too: three months after a playlist is deleted or a song is removed from one, and two months after an import finished, your server forgets those entries, and a song nobody has liked, listed, played or downloaded any more is removed in the same hour. An old import still shows its numbers (how many songs it found and which it did not), but no longer the list of songs. A phone that was away all that time is still told what is gone the next time it syncs, and a playlist you deleted stays deleted when you bring your library over from the music server again.
* Your phone now also forgets a song that nothing of yours uses any more when your server took the song's last place in a playlist away, also after the phone was away for months while the server cleaned up: before, the song's entry stayed in the phone's own list until the phone was set up again. A song you liked, keep in another playlist, downloaded or chose to keep on your phone stays, a downloaded song still plays offline, and the phone now reads your likes and playlists before the list of songs the server has removed (N-35).
