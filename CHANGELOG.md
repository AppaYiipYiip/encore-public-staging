# Changelog

User-visible changes, newest first. One line per change, in plain words.

## Unreleased

Everything below is built and tested but **not yet walked on a phone** (`docs/tasks/RELEASE-CANDIDATE.md`, the definition of pass); nothing here is released.

* `python -m encore_server --help` (or a word it does not know, like `restore` or a typo) now prints the commands and stops; before, it started a second server on your live data. Only no word runs the server.
* An edit you made while your server was away (a like, a playlist change) is now sent as soon as the server answers again, and a failed delivery is tried again by itself when its wait ends; before, it could sit for ten minutes until something else woke the app.
* A playlist you renamed, deleted, or took a song out of is no longer changed back on the screen while the change is still waiting to be sent: before, a change made on another device could bring the old name (or the removed song, or the deleted playlist) back for a minute until your change reached the server.
* A song is no longer called "gone for good" just because the server's song search had a bad moment. Before, when the search service timed out or was down, a tap answered "Nothing matching that song was found" as if it were final, a download gave the song up with no Retry button, and an imported playlist marked its songs as missing. Now a search that could not be made is a plain "try again" for all three, and the song is looked for again a few minutes later (B-122).
* Search now says when your phone is offline or cannot reach your server. The field says "Search your music", your own music is still found at once, and what is not downloaded is grey and says "Not downloaded". One quiet last line under the results says what more cannot be done, in the words of the reason: "More results need a connection." when the phone has no internet, "More results need your server." when only your server does not answer; a search that finds nothing in your music says so ("Nothing in your music matches ...", then "Connect to search more." or "Your server isn't answering, so only your music can be searched.") and never "No results" for what the phone simply cannot look for. The genre tiles stay but are dimmed, and a tap on one says "Not available right now" and "Browsing needs a connection." / "Browsing needs your server." with a way back to Search. Before, with no network the whole genre grid vanished, and with a network but a silent server Search kept showing the public catalogue's results as if nothing was wrong. All of it goes by itself when the connection is back. A server that goes silent only counts after four seconds (a blink of the network is not news), while having no network at all counts at once, as the grey rows already do (P1-16).
* A song's "..." menu now offers "Go to" for every artist of a song by several artists (one line each: "Go to Periphery", "Go to Opeth"), as the player already opens each artist on its own; a song with one artist keeps its single "Go to artist" line.
* There is a Data saver switch in Settings > Playback, one line under the two streaming-quality rows. It does something only while your phone is on mobile data (never on Wi-Fi, never when it is off): songs stream at Data saver quality (Opus 48 kbps) and new downloads are made at it, pictures are fetched small (also the ones kept on the phone for your downloaded songs), and only the next song is prepared ahead. Your own quality choices are never changed and are back when the switch goes off or you reach Wi-Fi; a download made on mobile data at the low quality is made again at your own quality, next to the old file, when the phone is next on Wi-Fi, and the old file is replaced only when the new one is complete. A person who chose Compatible (MP3) keeps it: Data saver never changes the format. Settings > Downloads & storage says so while it is limiting new downloads (only when you allowed downloads over mobile data; "Download over Wi-Fi only" is on by default). "Download over Wi-Fi only" is unchanged and now covered by a test: a download waits on mobile data while it is on and goes when it is off. A download whose refresh did not arrive no longer loses its old copy.

* The pictures of your downloaded songs now stay on your phone with them: their covers and the pictures of their artists are kept once each (five songs of one album by one artist are one cover and one picture), in a place that neither the app's own tidying of old pictures nor Android's "Clear cache" empties, and they are let go only when no downloaded song needs them any more. So offline, a downloaded song never turns into a letter.

* Songs that came from YouTube and have no picture anywhere else now show their video's thumbnail instead of a letter, also the ones you imported before: your phone is told about them once the next time your server looks for pictures, with no need to import the playlist again. Any real cover that is found later still replaces it.

* Search no longer stays empty while your phone is busy taking in music: while the library syncs (the first sync of a new phone, above all) on a slow phone, typing in Search could show nothing at all until the music stopped arriving, because every song that arrived made the search start over before it had finished. Search now shows what your library holds as soon as it has found it and never starts over for that; the guess for a band typed with a letter wrong is added to it when the list of bands is ready.
* An imported song that no catalogue has a picture for no longer shows a plain letter: it shows the thumbnail of its YouTube video, and a real cover still replaces it as soon as one is found (the server keeps the two apart, so it keeps looking for a better one).
* The songs your server already shows twice (once under "[unknown album]", once under the right album, and downloaded twice) are merged into one the next time the server starts: the copy that was saved first stays and takes your likes, playlist places, plays and saved queue, and the other is removed. Two songs that really are different (two different videos, or both in one of your playlists) are left alone. The server first keeps its own copy of its database, and a song that something still names by its old id keeps working. Phones that already synced keep showing the second copy until the app folds it in (the next line).
* A phone that already synced now folds the two copies into one when it next talks to the server that merged them: the second copy disappears from the lists and from the Songs total, your like, your places in playlists, your plays, your saved queue (with the place you were in the song) and the releases you saved on the phone follow the song that stays, and a song you had downloaded is kept on the phone under the new name (it is moved, never downloaded again; when both copies had been downloaded the one made at the higher quality stays and the other is deleted). A phone with nothing of the second copy changes nothing; a server from before this change works as it did.

* A song that your server saved is no longer shown twice after the server looks through its music again. The server used to describe a file it had already saved from the file's own tags a second time, and when the tags were poorer than the description it saved the file for (several artists written as one name, a length a few seconds apart, an "[Unknown Album]"), it made a second copy of the song for the same file, so it showed up twice and was downloaded twice. Now a file that already belongs to a song stays with that song and nothing new is made for it. Songs that were doubled before this change stay doubled until they are tidied up separately.
* A playlist now has a "Find in playlist" field under its title, as Spotify has: tap it, type a word, and the list shows only the songs of that playlist that match (by title, artist or album, found the way Search finds, so accents and other scripts work, and also with no connection). A tap on a found song plays it from its place in the playlist and the playlist goes on after it; the arrow or Back brings the whole playlist back, and the page is where you left it (N-25).
* A song you have just tapped can now be paused with the play/pause of a headset or the lock screen in the first moments, before it has started. Before, the player still showed Play for it, so that press asked for play and the song carried on. Now the player says it is loading from the tap on; while it loads the on-screen button is a spinner (nothing to press there) and the song starts when it is ready.

* Search finds a band in your library even when you get a letter or two wrong: "landmarks" finds Landmvrks and shows it as the Top result (a letter wrong, missing, extra or two next to each other swapped; two letters wrong from 9 letters on; never for names of 1 to 4 letters, which would match too many others), also with no network. It only looks for such a name when no artist of your library begins with what you typed, and it ranks below every exact answer.
* Search puts the band first when you type its name right: "jinjer" shows the band as the Top result, also when a song on your server has the band's name in its title ("Jinjer - Pisces (Official Video)", as a video list names it) or has no artist at all. Before, a song that only started with the words you typed could rank above the band and even above a song or an album that was exactly what you typed. A song or an album named exactly like your search still wins when the band with that name is barely known ("Hotel California" is still the Eagles' song), and one named exactly like it now comes before a song that merely starts with the same words (P1-07).
* A downloaded song starts the moment you tap it also when your phone has a network but your server does not answer (at home with the server off, on a Wi-Fi with no internet): the app used to ask your server first, which took about two seconds, before playing a file it already had. The same for Next. Songs that are not downloaded are still checked once and say so.
* Tapping the tab you are already on takes you back to its first page: Search is empty again as if nothing had been searched (the typed words are gone and the keyboard closes), Home and Your Library go back to showing everything and scroll to the top, and Your Library opens on the downloaded songs again when you have no connection. Going to another tab and coming back still keeps what you had typed.

* The song at the top of a search now has the same buttons as every song below it: the three dots (play next, add to queue, add to a playlist, like, download), a long press for the same menu, and a swipe to the right to add it to the queue or to the left to like it. A song that is not on your server yet still has no three dots anywhere in Search, the same as the songs below it (N-21).
* An artist's page now has Shuffle and Play side by side, as drawn, so you can look an artist up and just listen. On an artist your server has, both play every song of that artist that your server holds (Play from the first song, Shuffle in random order); without a connection they skip what is not downloaded. Before, Play started the catalogue's ten popular songs and there was no Shuffle. On an artist your server does not have yet, they play the Popular list on the page, one song fetched after the other; Shuffle puts that list in random order. Both are dimmed when there is nothing to play, and Play shows pause while that artist is playing. The artist's page also gets the three dots it is drawn with (play next, add to queue and add to a playlist for all of the artist's songs your server holds) (N-24).
* An artist's page you open again opens at once: what the catalogue said about the artist is kept for half an hour (it was asked again at every open, and the first look asked three times where one is enough), and the app now times how long each playlist and artist page takes to open so a slow one can be seen on a real phone.
* On a song by several artists, every artist is a tap of its own in the player (it was one tap for the whole line, which opened the first artist): "Forever" by Drake, Kanye West, Lil Wayne and Eminem opens Kanye West's page when you press Kanye West. The line of artists is always one line high and scrolls sideways when there are many, so the player does not move from one song to the next.
* Songs by several artists are saved with each artist on its own (an `ARTISTS` tag), so your music server links the song to every one of its artists instead of to an artist named "Post Malone, Swae Lee" that nobody can open. Songs already saved keep the old tag until they are re-tagged.
* Pressing an artist's name in the player (and an artist, an album or a playlist the phone does not have) no longer leaves a spinner for ever: a song saved with several artists ("Post Malone, Swae Lee") belonged, for your music server, to an artist that the phone never receives; the phone now links such a song to the artist of its album, the page opens the real artist, and a page that cannot be filled says so and keeps its Back button.
* Pictures show: the app asked your server for every picture with a ticket whose end time it had worked out from the phone's clock, the server had written its own, and a clock one second apart was enough for the server to refuse every picture (artists and new albums stayed letters; only the ones fetched in the first minutes showed). The address now carries the server's own time, and a ticket the server refuses is replaced and the pictures ask again.
* A song your server cannot find on YouTube no longer leaves the player spinning for ever: the player asks the server why, says it in the server's words ("Couldn't play Lofi For Focus: Nothing matching that song was found."), does not try the same song a second time, stops spinning, and Play tries once more.
* The next songs of your queue are made ready while a song plays: the moment you tap a song (and as an album's next songs are queued behind it) the app tells your Encore server which two songs come next, and the server looks them up and keeps their stream address (no audio, paced like all background asking, at most 40 songs an hour), so Next finds them at once instead of waiting 2 to 3 s (P1-04, audit B-107). Songs you downloaded are not named, and nothing changes on a server that is not an Encore server.
* The full player now has what Spotify's has: where the sound goes at the bottom left (tap it for a sheet with this phone and Android's own list of speakers, headphones and Bluetooth), a Share button for the song (with its YouTube link when Encore knows it), and the Lyrics card is always there, the same size, so the screen no longer jumps between a song with lyrics and one without.
* There are no 30-second previews any more: every song you tap, from Search, an album or an artist, plays in full, and a server that cannot play a song says so in words instead of playing a snippet. Lyrics now also load for songs that are only streamed (not yet saved).
* Songs with several artists ("Beanie Sigel, Freeway") that Encore had saved on your server were reported as "not found" by the music server and never became playable from your library; they are found now.
* Lyrics that your phone kept as plain text are looked at again after an hour for a time-synced copy (so a song that stayed untappable, like Opeth's "Benighted", gets its tappable lines on the next play).
* Lyrics: when the lyrics service holds a song as plain text, Encore now also looks for a time-synced copy of the same song (so you can tap a line to jump to it), and text that was saved in the wrong character set ("weÂ´ll" for "we´ll") is repaired.
* A download that has songs that did not come now says so on its page ("12 songs did not come", with the first three and why), so a Retry button never stands alone; the Download button answers the moment you press it; artists in Your Library that the music server has no photo of show the cover of one of their songs instead of initials; and a long playlist is less busy while the server is still saving it (the phone looks for new songs at most every 10 seconds and no longer redraws a page that did not change).
* Importing a playlist now reads up to 10,000 songs (it stopped at 2000 without saying so, and a 2,500-song playlist showed 2000); when a list is still longer than that the import card says so.
* Songs up to 2 hours 30 minutes can now be saved on your server (it was 45 minutes), so a 2-hour recitation or a long concert is kept and can be downloaded to a phone; and a download that is refused part-way through a long file asks for that piece again instead of starting the whole file over.
* Saving an imported playlist on your server is now as fast as you choose: the server waits 4 seconds on average between its background requests to YouTube (the Host can set 3 to 8 in `.env`, `ENCORE_YOUTUBE_PACE_S`). A 200-song playlist takes about 10 minutes at 3 seconds, 13 at 4 and 27 at 8 (measured: 8 min 40 s at 1.5 s with two workers, nothing failed, YouTube refused nothing); a song you tap is never made to wait for it (D-67).
* Importing a YouTube Music playlist no longer searches YouTube for every song: the songs play, and are saved on your server, from the video the playlist points at. Before, a 288-song import made about 50 searches in its first five minutes, a song you tapped before its turn was searched at the tap, and saving a 200-song playlist took about an hour and a half instead of about forty minutes.
* If the app is closed or killed while a song is playing (the system ending it, a swipe from the recent apps), it now comes back within 2 seconds of where you were. Before, it could be up to 15 seconds off, and a jump you had just made inside the song was forgotten: seeking to 2:28 and being killed 2 seconds later brought the song back at 4:01.
* Updating the server (the command encore update) after a small change now downloads a few megabytes instead of about 240: the server's libraries are kept in a layer of their own that only changes when the libraries do. On a slow line the last update took over 7 minutes.
* The full player now uses the whole screen, whatever the shape of the phone: on a tall phone the title, the controls and the queue button sit together at the bottom with the cover in the room above, instead of leaving an empty area under the controls; on a short phone with big text the page scrolls and nothing is cut off; on a foldable opened up or a phone held on its side the cover goes beside the controls.
* The installer and the install guide no longer say your music folder is "only read". What is true: Encore never changes your own files; songs you keep from the web are saved in a folder called Encore inside it, and the installer now says so when it asks where your music is.
* Behind the scenes: when many people use one server at the same moment, looking at your library or your playlists no longer waits for other people's changes to finish. With twenty simulated people the slowest wait fell from about 6 seconds to under a second; two changes still take turns, but each is short and nothing slow happens inside one.
* Behind the scenes: the server writes about 18 times faster (each change no longer pays for closing and re-opening its database), so many people changing things at once wait less.
* After a list has played to its end, pressing Play (on the album page, the mini player, the lock screen or a headset) now starts it over from the first song, and when it ends again the player stops and shows Play. Before, Play replayed the last song once and then showed Pause with no sound.
* On the server, pressing Download on a playlist or on your liked songs can now ask the server for the songs it does not have yet, ahead of other people's imports; a single tap on a song still comes first, and a long download can no longer push another person's tap behind it. Saving a release and Retry follow the same rule.
* On your phone, pressing Download on a playlist or on your liked songs now asks your server to get the songs it does not have yet, ahead of other people's imports, and the phone keeps checking while the app is open.
* Under a download (in Library, on the playlist or album page, and in Settings > Downloads & storage) you now see how far BOTH stages have got: "12 of 217 on this phone · 40 ready on your server", "Waiting its turn on your server", "your server is resting until 21:40", or why your phone waits ("waiting for Wi-Fi", "waiting for a connection", "waiting for your server", or that downloads are paused because your storage limit is reached or your phone is almost full), with a bar of two shades. Songs that did not come are named with a reason and a Retry button. The notification no longer says "Downloads finished" when your server still has songs to hand over.
* When a song cannot be saved to the server, a friend now sees a short plain reason ("Not found.", "Needs an age check.") and never the server's internal error text or folder names; the person who runs the server still sees the cause.
* The server can now say how far it is with keeping every playlist, album and your liked songs for download, in one answer: how many songs it has ready, how many it is fetching or has queued, and which ones it could not get and why (to you as the person who runs the server, with the cause; to a friend, in plain words). The app does not show it yet (a phone can also ask to be told as it changes, so the numbers move by themselves). A song that failed to save is no longer forgotten after a week while someone still has it in a playlist or liked.
* Offline, the app now keeps showing what you cannot play after the "You're offline" line has gone: Home, the Library and the song lists show it grey with "Not downloaded", Home lists what is on your phone under "Downloaded", and it all goes away by itself when the connection is back. When Android finds internet but your server does not answer, the line says "Can't reach your server."; with no internet (no data, a sign-in page) it still says "You're offline". A tap on a song that is not downloaded says which of the two it is.
* The first time you press Download, Encore asks once, in plain words, whether it may show how far your downloads have got (Android 13 and newer); say "Not now" and it never asks again, and the download goes ahead either way. No news or alerts are sent unless you switch them on in Settings.
* Songs the music server could not file under an album no longer show up as an album called "[Unknown Album]" with a grey cover: Home leaves that out, and the lists, the artist page, the player and the lock screen show them as songs, with the one name "Songs without an album" where a name is needed.
* An Encore server never offers the old "Get new music" request page, whatever the "Play songs your server does not have" switch says.
* The music server's door on the internet now slows wrong passwords like every other door (a stranger could try 65 a second); an app that sends only a plain password cannot sign in through the public address.
* A server that does not answer no longer makes the player skip songs: it waits and plays on when the server is back.
* One calm line, once, when the phone is offline or the server is away; "another server answers" gets its own line.
* A tap plays the full song, never a silent 30-second preview; the build says which build it is (About and Diagnostics).
* Downloads: a downloaded album or playlist is one entry that survives a restart; removing a playlist takes its songs off the phone at once; a song two downloaded releases share stays until both are gone; a per-song Download and Remove download in the song menu.
* Add to library and Download on a release are done by the server and show only what it confirmed.
* Long-press and a three-dots menu on songs and Library rows; one "my music / whole server" switch.
* Home offers Jam only where the server has Jam; the avatar opens a drawer; "Share what I'm listening to" only where it works; no speed control or Private listening in the player.
* Invitations: the link opens a page in the browser, a join whose answer was lost can be repeated, and a removed or locked person's phones, songs and pictures stop working.
* Wrong passwords are slowed and never lock an account; request sizes are limited; the server no longer publishes its route list.
* The first sign-in finds out within seconds that it is an Encore server; "Invite someone" and People appear once the server is known.
* The server installs in one command, backs itself up every night, and updates its own yt-dlp safely.
* The repository was reset: a clean history, GPL-3.0 licence.
